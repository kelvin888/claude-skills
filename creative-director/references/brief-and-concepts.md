# Templates — Creative Brief, Concepts, Storyboard

Decisive over exhaustive. A brief that tries to say everything decides nothing. Every line
below should be a *choice* you could defend, not a hedge.

---

## Creative Brief  →  `creative-direction/01-creative-brief.md`

```markdown
# Creative Brief — [Project]

**Mission** — why this exists, in one sentence.
**Primary audience** — the one person we're designing for, and their mindset on arrival.
**Positioning** — the one differentiating truth, stated as something provable.
**Core message** — the single idea a visitor leaves with. (If they remember one thing…)
**Emotional goal** — the one feeling, and where in the journey it peaks.
**Brand personality** — 3–5 adjectives, plus a one-line "never" (what we won't do/say).

**References** — real sites by name, each with *what specifically to borrow*:
- [Site] — for its [scroll feel / restraint / type / lighting / sound], NOT its [skin].
- … (3–6 references; mix interaction studios with a left-field one for tension)

**Interaction philosophy** — the rule of thumb for how it responds. e.g. "Reveal, don't
appear. Transform, don't replace. Everything responds to cursor and scroll velocity."
**Motion philosophy** — the feeling of movement. e.g. "Heavy, physical, with anticipation —
nothing snaps; everything has weight and follow-through."

**Success metrics** — how we'll know it worked (qualitative + quantitative).
**Things to avoid** — the explicit no-list (generic SaaS hero, stocky 3D, autoplay sound,
parallax-for-its-own-sake, anything that fights the one feeling).
```

End the brief by asking for explicit approval. Do not design past an unapproved brief.

## Concepts  →  `creative-direction/02-concepts.md`

Present **3–5**, genuinely distinct. The test: if you swapped the names, could you tell them
apart? If not, they're the same concept in different paint.

```markdown
## Concept [A] — "[Evocative name]"
**The idea** — one line.
**The feeling** — the dominant emotion and atmosphere.
**Core interaction metaphor** — the one mechanic the whole site is built around
  (e.g. "the product assembles from particles as you scroll", "a single continuous camera
  push through one 3D space", "the cursor is a light source revealing the page").
**Why it works for this brief** — trace it to the audience, the message, the emotion.
**What it risks** — the honest downside (performance, build cost, could feel cold, etc.).
**Closest reference** — the one site that proves this is achievable.
```

Make them pull in different directions on purpose — e.g.:
- **The Reveal** — mystery, darkness, information withheld and earned.
- **The Machine** — precision and systems; motion *is* the proof of capability.
- **The Human** — warm, slow, cinematic; people over product.
- **The Artifact** — one hero object, lit and rotated like a museum piece.

Close with your **recommendation and reasoning**, then hand the choice to the user. Gate:
one concept selected before storyboarding.

## Storyboard  →  `creative-direction/03-storyboard.md`

Scenes, not sections. Borrow film structure: each scene earns the next.

```markdown
# Storyboard — "[Chosen concept]"

## Scene 1 — [name]  (the hook)
- **Purpose** — what this scene must accomplish in the arc.
- **What the visitor sees** — the staging, in plain language.
- **What they feel / understand** — the one takeaway.
- **Transition out** — how we hand off to the next scene (cut, morph, camera push, wipe).

## Scene 2 — [name]  (rising curiosity / the tension)
…
## Scene 3 — [name]  (the reveal — the product / the point)
## Scene 4 — [name]  (the proof / transformation)
## Scene 5 — [name]  (the call to action — the only place we ask)
```

A typical arc: **Hook → Tension → Reveal → Proof → Call to action.** Compress for a short
page (Hook → Reveal → CTA). The point is that scrolling feels like *progression through a
story*, not paging through a brochure. Gate: arc approved before motion/visual spec.
